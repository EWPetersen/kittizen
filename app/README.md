# Kittizen

This document supersedes the first-pass map limitations in the historical project review.

## Start and update

From `C:\kittizen`, run ` .\app\start.ps1 ` and open http://127.0.0.1:8077. The launcher prefers the bundled Python on this computer, avoiding the Windows Store `python` alias. The HTTP app still uses only Python's standard library and SQLite.

Extraction requires two small libraries, installed on this computer already. On a fresh checkout, install them once with ` .\app\setup-extractor.ps1 `. Versions are pinned in `scripts/requirements.txt`; there are no frontend packages or build steps.

The normal launcher checks the game at startup and the running service watches it every 30 seconds. Two stable samples are required after an archive/manifest change. Extraction runs in a worker process; the existing app keeps serving the last good map. A successful atomic publish reloads the backend and open browsers automatically. No server restart or manual browser refresh is required after a patch. If the app was closed during the patch, startup catches up. This is an in-app watcher, not an OS-wide background service.

To force a rebuild: `python app/scripts/update_game_data.py --force`. To select PTU, supply `-GamePath 'D:\Games\Roberts Space Industries\StarCitizen\PTU'` to `start.ps1`. `-NoGameWatch` is available for isolated testing.

## Map behavior

**Activity heatmap:** always visible in 2D and 3D, with right-drag panning and normal camera rotation. Recent Interdiction/Pirates reports contribute danger heat, Suspicious reports contribute 60%, and Clear reports contribute none. Nearby reports add together on a fixed intensity scale. Every contribution halves after 30 minutes and disappears at two hours from creation; verification cannot renew it. Heat and cached report expiry update every 15 seconds, including during a failed network refresh. System views show activity through the current camera; universe discs show per-system totals. Heat is scoped to the chosen shard and activity filter. No heat is not evidence of safety.

- Default universe overview: three **separate** orbital discs for Stanton, Pyro and Nyx, placed using their extracted galactic positions. Each disc uses its own readable local scale; galactic coordinates are not mixed with local metre coordinates.
- Click a disc, use **Map system**, or zoom toward a disc to enter a system. Zooming out far enough returns to the universe.
- Stanton's reset orientation places **microTech upper right**, Crusader left and ArcCorp lower right.
- **Drag to rotate** the 3D camera. **Right-drag or Shift-drag to pan**. Scroll/pinch to zoom. One touch rotates; two touches pan and pinch.
- **Double right-click** or use **Back** to step out: location → parent body → local planet orbits → system → universe. The route is preserved. Double-click a jump portal to visit an installed destination.
- Selecting a planet from a system first frames its local moons and nearby stations. Selecting it again fits the whole globe inside the frame; moons open directly in a fitted body view. Surface/orbital buttons remain available. POIs face their actual surface position; far-side markers are occluded in close body views.
- Local orbital views keep city/station labels selectable while deferring facility artwork and glyphs, so they cannot cover planet/moon art. Facility models become available in the next, closer body view.
- Selections ease the camera into place; dragging or zooming interrupts the movement. Reduced-motion preferences skip the animation. Click an activity card, its heading, location, or map marker to focus the reported position, including surface POIs.
- Globes preserve native texture colours. Label order is planets, gates, stars, primary local/system hubs, moons, then secondary navigation detail where space allows. Labels avoid controls, report markers and each other, with stable anchors and visibility hysteresis.
- All 31 planets and moons have six searchable, selectable OM markers (186 total), generated by the extraction pipeline from body axes and quantum boundaries. Search for a body name plus `OM`; selecting an OM zooms to it with its parent body in context. OM numbering remains approximate.
- Orbital stations, comm arrays and mapped facilities use shared low-detail game geometry where available. Models load on demand, rotate with the camera and use byte-bounded raster caches. These are map/exterior components, not full interiors or full material reconstructions.

### Repeatable map-model extraction

Run `app/scripts/setup_map_converter.ps1` once on this Windows PC. It downloads [Cryengine Converter v2.0.0](https://github.com/Markemp/Cryengine-Converter/releases/tag/v2.0.0) from its upstream release and checks the pinned SHA-256. The existing game update pipeline then extracts only referenced starmap CGF/CGFM pairs, chooses a low-detail mesh, converts it and publishes compact content-hashed geometry alongside the catalogue. Raw models and intermediate glTF remain in ignored local storage; the converter remains in `.devtools/`. It does not modify game files.

The map extracts CGF/CGFM and CGA/CGAM navigation geometry, including landing-zone symbols, plus selected verified exterior components for Levski and Klescher. Position-only meshes weld duplicate material-seam vertices and drop unused vertices. Raw conversions stay local. Procedural portal artwork replaces generic jump-gate glyphs while retaining game coordinates and destinations.

Physical station/POI selection centres the target with a 3.5× context pullback; an orbital station keeps its parent body's limb behind it. Nearby giant globes render only the visible viewport. Moving globe rasters use at most 144px (112px viewport crops); idle close-ups refine to 384/512px. Small overview globes use 48/96px rasters. Models use 160px during motion; settled sprites use 128/256/384/768px according to their projected size. Detailed source images remain visible through base-texture fallback while loading and are requested only after motion. Full decoded globe sources have a 24 MiB budget, tiny fallback mips share another 2 MiB, globe/model rasters each have an 8 MiB budget, and only two image decodes run concurrently. Cached heat fields are reused when the view/activity is unchanged; moving heat fields use a coarser grid. V3 art remains lazy-loaded and independent.

Map selection exposes Depart here / Route here / Reset pins above the canvas. A/B pins can be chosen in either order; a route is plotted only when both different endpoints exist. Newer route requests supersede stale responses. Navigation and Intelligence edge tabs collapse/restore desktop panels with device-local preferences; on phones the same tabs open/close the existing mobile panels. Labels follow body movement immediately, retain anchor choices across temporary hiding, ease anchor changes only when collision-free, and settle without an idle render loop.

Star appearance comes from each world container's `SunLight` overrides, decoded with the pinned StarBreaker installed by `scripts/setup_v2_extractor.ps1`. The shared navigation hologram material is not used as the physical star definition. The current world radii are Stanton 696,000,000 m, Pyro 570,720,000 m and Nyx 765,600,000 m; each has its own material, surface intensity and inherited light colour. Nyx's referenced F2V noise texture is converted to a shared 512px WebP and sampled at the material's tiling. Other stars retain a cached procedural surface. The browser approximates emissive shading and corona, with orbital-disc light using the world light colour. All stars share a common focus span so their actual size differences remain visible; overview glyphs have proportional minimum sizes. Routing obstruction radii remain independent. No continuous animation loop is added.

Ruin Station (`RR_P6_LEO`), Orbituary (`RR_P3_LEO`) and Checkmate (`RR_P2_L4`) remain Pyro primary hubs; Nyx keeps Levski and the four People's Service Stations primary. Their labels follow planets and gates, ahead of secondary details. Stanton's city/orbital hubs become prominent in the selected planet's local view.

Spawn destinations are primary across systems: Nyx includes Levski and the four People's Service Stations (Alpha, Delta, Theta, Lambda); Stanton includes its four cities, Grim HEX, orbital hab stations and Lagrange rest stops. This is a curated policy in `build_catalog.primary_location`, matched against game record names; it does not infer spawn permissions from decorative station names. Nyx breaker stations/transit points and Pyro's other service stops remain secondary.

V3 artwork uses ship images and explicitly referenced `EntityUIDisplayParams.displayIcon` textures from the game's loadout-icon directory. Shared source images become shared content-hashed WebP files, lazy-loaded in build slots, comparisons, fitting, acquisition and reference views. The catalogue records the source and distinguishes ship artwork from loadout icons. Missing images remain explicit; many ship components have no thumbnail reference, and skin variants may share a base icon. The current pipeline includes 320 weapon and 1,764 equipment records with icons in addition to 237 ship/vehicle records with artwork. It does not yet extract full ship/equipment assemblies into an interactive 3D viewer.

Recent visual reference: [2026 gameplay guide, starmap chapter at 50:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SLJWeA4byZM&t=3000s), reviewed November 1, 2026; the page showed publication three months earlier. [Recent community map discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/starcitizen/comments/1vptuxj/question_for_the_ui_team/) supplied navigation/zoom feedback, not a substitute for visual footage. Installed game references remain authoritative for the extracted material values.

Unknown transforms, invalid geometry or failed conversions stop publication and retain the previous catalogue. If a future game patch changes the mesh format, update and validate the pinned converter deliberately. The hosted app only serves the generated geometry; it does not run the converter.
- Real game starmap textures are mapped onto globes using the extracted reference rotations. The renderer draws on demand and uses a lower interactive resolution while dragging, sharpening when released.

## POI-to-POI routes

Search the origin and select **Depart here**, then search the destination and select **Route here**. This immediately plans the full route. The departure/destination lists and saved jumps also work with POIs and with different systems.

The backend is authoritative. It uses extracted obstruction, arrival and sub-point radii. Surface departure/arrival is an amber **local flight** leg; quantum legs are green. A visibility graph chooses among six reference-axis OM candidates when a direct leg is obstructed. Routes through different systems use reciprocal installed gates and include explicit jump-tunnel legs. Clicking an itinerary step frames that segment. Saved jumps retain their actual endpoint IDs.

Report placement measures distance along those planned flight legs; it never interpolates across unrelated system coordinate frames. Tunnel length is excluded. A report is assigned to its actual system as well as its shard. Reputation, independent account verification and two-hour report expiry remain in place.

These are **estimated companion routes**, not CIG's complete navigation solver. OM numbering/reference phase and the conservative local-flight model need comparison with live gameplay. Drive range, fuel, quantum boost, spool/calibration, live planet rotation, event state and dynamic navigation restrictions are not simulated. Verify the final route in game.

## Extracted coverage and maintenance

The tested LIVE archive produced **773 catalogue entries across all three systems**, **32 texture mappings**, and **108 traversed containers**. This includes surface facilities, landing zones, orbital stations, jump points and Levski. Hidden and auxiliary entries are retained where needed for hierarchy but do not all appear as default map markers. File-backed conditional event points are not a promise that an event is active on a shard.

The extractor reads the P4K index selectively, the DataCore v8 navigation schema, English localization and relevant nested containers. It reconstructs only the necessary DDS mipmaps, rather than unpacking the entire 161 GB game archive. Local game files remain read-only. Generated data and game images stay in ignored storage/asset folders. Unknown formats, incomplete systems or changed files during extraction retain the last good publication and expose an update error in the app.

Main files: `server.py` (API/storage/watcher), `routing.py` (constraints), `public/app.js` (UI), `public/map.js` (camera/map), `public/sphere.js` (globe sampling), and `scripts/` (extraction). Generated asset paths are immutable per game/pipeline version. The visual shell remains custom; it does not reproduce every CIG shader, animation, interior or game-only UI interaction.

## Accounts and reputation

Reports are isolated by shard. Authors cannot vote on their own reports; an account can verify each report once. One latest contribution per verifier per author counts toward reputation, with a seven-day evidence half-life. Score = 100 × positive weight / (positive weight + negative weight + 4). Established starts at 40%; Trusted requires 70% and ten weighted positive verifiers. These are local accounts, not verified RSI identities.

For a phone on your private network, explicitly launch with -BindAddress 0.0.0.0 and open your PC's LAN address on port 8077. The normal default is loopback only.




Shard selection uses one of US, EU, AUS, ASIA and exactly three ASCII digits, stored as REGION-NNN. Leading zeros are preserved. Old free-form shard data is retained without guessing a region or number; invalid saved selections prompt a fresh selection.


## Pilot profiles and optional Google sign-in

Click your username to open your profile, change your public handle, review lifetime contribution totals and your latest 50 reports, or sign out. Report authors link to public profiles at `/#pilot/HANDLE`; only the owner receives detailed report history. Google email, name, picture and credentials are never published. Trust remains based on independent report verification, not Google identity.

Google is disabled until configured. To enable it:

1. Create a Google Cloud project and configure its OAuth consent screen/audience. Create an **OAuth client ID → Web application** using [Google's setup guide](https://developers.google.com/identity/gsi/web/guides/get-google-api-clientid). Add your exact website origin under Authorized JavaScript origins. For development, use `http://localhost:8077` and access the app at that address; for deployment use the final HTTPS origin. Add test users while the consent screen is in testing mode. No client secret or redirect URI is needed for this GIS popup credential flow.
2. Install the optional dependency into the same Python environment used by the server: `python -m pip install -r app/requirements-auth.txt`.
3. Set `KITTIZEN_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` to the public client ID ending in `.apps.googleusercontent.com`, then restart the app. On HTTPS deployments also set `KITTIZEN_SECURE_COOKIE=1`.
4. Existing pilots should sign in locally, open their profile and select **Link Google sign-in**. New pilots use **Continue with Google** in the sign-in dialog and can choose a handle from their profile. Linking preserves reports, saved routes and trust; accounts are never silently merged by email.

The backend uses Google's verification library to check the signature, issuer, expiry and audience, then checks a ten-minute, single-use nonce. Google `sub` identifies the account. An existing Google identity cannot be linked to a different pilot. No Google access/refresh tokens are stored. Google scripts load only when sign-in/linking is opened and a client ID is configured. Local login remains available during setup or Google outages.

Tests cover profiles, owner-only history, totals including expired reports, Google-disabled errors, rejected tokens, nonce matching/replay, returning accounts and account-link conflicts. Google responses are mocked in these isolated tests. A real Google popup and token verification must still be tested after the client ID and origins are configured.

V1 interdiction planning: plot the target route, then choose **Plan interception** on the map or in Navigate. Select one quantum leg; local flight and jump tunnels are excluded. Add up to five additional origins, or include the departure's moons. `public/interdiction.js` encloses projected uncertainty discs in a common cross-section and finds an offset centre within the extracted snare radius, retaining the chosen safety margin. A placement slider moves between the closest and furthest validated positions. The cross-section diagram shows radius usage, not catch probability. Body exclusion uses the largest of four body radii, the extracted QT obstruction radius or arrival radius, plus snare radius and margin, conservatively expanded for an offset centre. This is a heuristic, not measured physics-grid or armistice clearance. Minimum QT travel defaults to an explicitly editable 10,000 km community assumption. Distance inputs support grouped km, Mm and Gm; switching units preserves the physical distance. No live target telemetry or drive-timing prediction is provided.

Navigation uses three non-collinear same-system centre markers selected independently from the installed catalogue. Three-range sphere intersection preserves both mirrored solutions; degenerate triangles, inconsistent ranges and non-finite inputs are rejected. A 1 km range residual tolerance is not a position-error bound: distant-marker rounding can amplify into kilometres of position uncertainty. Same-system X/Y/Z metre coordinates offer a manual alternative, with cross-track error and correction to the planned point. Where geometry permits, the planner validates a two-jump setup guide against the route service; otherwise it explicitly asks for coordinate navigation. Signed-in pilots can save up to 50 private interception configurations and reload them against current catalogue data. They are never posted as community activity. A plan remains available when switching between the map and its dialog; changing the target route, resetting pins or refreshing game data clears the current calculation. SnarePlan research informed the workflow; Chase, Smart Assist and belt-turn planning are not implemented here.
